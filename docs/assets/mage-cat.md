# Nekueda Mage Cat in Cat Nest

Purchased listing: https://www.fab.com/listings/710bc3f3-6bfd-4942-9eea-c7af4901bf03
Author: Nekueda. License: Fab Standard License. The original package includes a NoAI restriction. The model is used as an authored game asset, with deterministic format conversion and texture resizing. No generative processing or training uses the licensed model, its textures or the package artwork. The separate original illustrations for the expression picker are generated from a text-only prompt; see `cat-expressions-v1.json`.

The private local sources are under `artifacts/licensed/mage-cat/`, which is ignored by Git. Source FBX, Unity package, texture archive, and documentation must not enter the public source repository. Only the curated optimized assets live in `stevencheninfilm/cat-nest-data/assets/mage-cat-v1/`. The backend serves a fixed allowlist for the game; it cannot fetch arbitrary files or expose source packages, room files, credentials, or upstream errors.

`prepare-mage-cat.mjs` preserves all three authored LODs, the skeleton and the nine non-TPose animations. It merges identical vertices, normalizes FBX weights to the four-influence web renderer, and optimizes redundant animation keys. Textures are resized to 1024 pixels for the coat and 512 for the facial planes and wand; their authored artwork and transparent face layers are retained. The default model is the author's black cat, purple outfit, amber eyes and yellow wand. The exported geometry/animations are 4,611,856 bytes; all 43 game files total 5,871,920 bytes.

The original body and clothing variants remain independently selectable, alongside three eye colors and three wand colors. The appearance is one validated cloud action (`dress`) and produces no currency or growth reward. Existing version-one saves receive the original default appearance while retaining progress and history. Canceling the editor restores the saved appearance. Successful edits become visible to both roles after a GitHub commit.

The home behavior controller (`src/cat-behavior.js`) derives mood from the current decayed hunger, mood and energy values, with explicit sleep taking priority. It uses local device time for morning (06:00–11:00), day (11:00–18:00), evening (18:00–22:00) and night (22:00–06:00). This is local presentation; autonomous behavior never changes sleeping, care quotas, growth, currency or cloud history.

Seven authored clips participate in care and spontaneous gestures: Idle, Walk, Run, Turn Left, Turn Right, Attack and Special Attack. Between 5–13 second quiet gaps, an awake cat may look around, tread in place, nuzzle or wink; a happy, energetic cat can also run, practice a wand swipe or perform a little magic. These lively idle starts are separated by at least 45 seconds, with per-clip gaps of 50 seconds for Run, 60 seconds for Attack and 90 seconds for Special Attack. Care and manual choices also inform this recent-motion memory. Weighted choices avoid recent gesture IDs and original clip families where alternatives exist; smaller reaction sets can revisit an earlier choice without falling into a fixed three-action cycle. Fatigue and night extend gaps to 12–24 seconds, suppress lively gestures and add yawning or temporary dozing. The room and model lighting also soften at night. Temporary dozing is visual only and does not spend a rest allowance or claim sleep rewards.

Petting and food use authored facial textures plus small outer-group movements, with curious turns during petting and occasional happy steps after eating. Low mood favors comfort and gentle walking; fatigue favors quiet responses. Happy, energetic play varies between wand magic (Special Attack), a playful wand swipe (Attack), and a short chase (Run). Calm play favors walking or turns with an occasional wand swipe. Hungry, tired and nighttime play stays gentle. Touching the cat while all care actions are cooling down still gives a local facial response, without granting another reward. Dialogue also follows needs and time. Blink timing, gesture gaps and playback rates vary rather than following fixed cycles. Hurt and Death carry an explicit manual-only flag and are excluded from autonomous and daily-care selection.

`src/mage-cat-player.js` plays original tracks unchanged, repeats locomotion when needed, uses the actual clip duration and playback speed for completion, and blends back to Idle. Repeated interactions can restart the same clip; faded actions are stopped. Manual rotation yields to the user's drag. Hidden views pause and clear pending gestures rather than replaying a backlog. Reduced motion keeps the cat on the slowed Idle clip while preserving expression feedback. The court avatar opts out of autonomous home gestures.

All procedural movements are assigned to the outer group on each frame; they never change the author's facial attachment nodes or bone rotations, preventing accumulated pose drift and clipping between face, skin, hair and hat. Death and Hurt are available as explicit performances in the palette; neither is used for natural sleep or unattended care. The renderer chooses a single LOD, caps resolution and runs at 30 fps. For manual verification, use the existing shared preview at `http://127.0.0.1:5174/tests/cat-behavior-preview.html`; the scenario controls do not write to the game save. `tests/cat-behavior.test.mjs` covers mood/time boundaries, five-minute unattended behavior, care priority, safe sleep, reduced motion, clip timing and playback cleanup.

The home's “表情” thought-bubble launcher opens a transparent glass palette with six expressions: happy, calm, lonely, hungry, tired and sleeping. Selecting a face closes the palette immediately, shows that expression once for three seconds, then restores the cat's natural mood or sleep state. Live need updates are retained during the expression; no care values or rewards are changed. A new choice replaces the previous preview, while accepted care, cancellation or backgrounding clears it safely. The palette's greeting also closes the window and plays a local touch response. The launcher sits at the upper right of the cat. The 248-pixel glass palette opens alongside it when space permits, and below the cat on narrower screens; it stays within viewport bounds. The avatar retains its normal size. The original UI portraits use a transparent 3×2 PNG atlas generated with the built-in image tool from text only.

The same compact palette has an “动作” page exposing all nine original animations: Idle, Walk, Run, Attack, Special Attack, Hit, Death, Turn Left and Turn Right. `src/cat-actions.js` maps the listing's Hit to the purchased clip named Hurt. Selecting an action closes the palette, plays its untouched skeleton tracks once at the original speed and blends back to the current natural mood or real sleep. Walk and Run use one full cycle; Death holds its final pose for half a second before returning. The UI uses Chinese-only names, including “吓一跳” for Hurt and “装晕” for Death. Action thumbnails are 58 pixels (50 on short viewports), with compact cards, a single-line header and a combined footer hint. All action choices are local presentation with no care cost, rewards or save writes. Repeated choices restart the selected clip, a new choice replaces the previous one, and care or backgrounding cancels it. Reduced motion substitutes the slowed Idle with the chosen facial feedback. Automated tests cover all nine mappings, duration, finite playback, interruptions, sleep restoration and releasing faded actions.

The action palette uses a new original transparent 3×3 action atlas and a transparent illustrated header from the built-in image tool, generated from text only. Both files live under `src/assets/cat-actions/v1/`; the full prompts and provenance are in `cat-actions-v1.json`. These are separate UI illustrations; the licensed model, textures and package artwork are not image-generation inputs. Live labels and controls remain HTML/CSS. Manual-only actions have a “点选” badge and explanatory caption. The glass effect, thought-bubble location, immediate close-on-selection and small popup width are retained.

To reproduce conversion, use `node scripts/prepare-mage-cat.mjs <private-source-dir> <private-output-dir>` with three and sharp available. `SHARP_MODULE_ROOT` may point to an installed bundled Node dependency directory. Do not execute scripts or editor extensions from downloaded packages.

For local development, `npm run dev:client` or `npm run dev` serves the curated files from `artifacts/licensed/mage-cat/processed/` directly through Vite at `/api/pet-model/mage-cat-v1/`. No separate port-9095 asset process or GitHub credentials are needed. The development middleware uses the same fixed game-file allowlist as production; it does not expose source archives. If the local optimized model is absent, the normal API proxy remains in use. Production builds keep their configured model API unchanged.
